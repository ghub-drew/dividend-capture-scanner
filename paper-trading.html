Paper Trading

Testing With Simulated Money

Before real dollars are at risk, the strategy gets run forward in time with pretend money in real market conditions. This page is the plan for that test, and the honest record of how it goes.

📊 Current Status

NOT STARTED YET

The scanner's numbers are currently validated by backtesting: every rebound time, best entry day, and drop ratio is measured from two years of real price history. What has NOT happened yet is forward testing, where the plan is executed day by day with simulated money and the results are compared against the predictions. That is what this page tracks.

🧪 What Is Paper Trading?

Paper trading means running the strategy right now, on today's actual market, but with fake money. A simulated account starts with pretend cash. Real orders are placed through a real broker's system, they fill against live market prices, the dividends arrive on schedule, and the account balance moves exactly as a real one would. The only difference is that no real dollars are at risk.

At the end of each trade, the real outcome is compared with what the scanner predicted. That comparison is the whole point.

🔍 Why the Backtest Is Not Enough

Backtesting looks backward and quietly assumes perfect conditions. Paper trading discovers the things the backtest cannot see:

Hidden costWhat it means
The spreadThe gap between the buying and selling price. The backtest uses one closing price; real trades pay the spread twice, once in and once out.
SlippageThe order fills at a slightly worse price than expected, especially on thinly-traded names.
Late reboundsA stock that historically recovered in 4 days might take 12 this time, tying up the capital and pushing back the next trade.
Dividend changesA company can cut or cancel a dividend after the plan was built around it.
Order problemsAn order that does not fill at all, or only partially, before the ex-date cutoff.
Why this matters here more than usual: dividend capture earns pennies per share. A strategy targeting roughly 1% of the capital per month has a thin margin, and the costs above are exactly the kind that can quietly eat a thin margin. With this strategy, execution quality is not a detail, it is the strategy.

🗺️ The Plan, In Detail

Paper trading doubles as the first step of Stage 2, because Interactive Brokers gives every account a free simulated mode that uses the identical connection as live trading. The automation gets built once and later flips from pretend money to real money with a switch. The work breaks into four phases.

Phase 0: Account setup (one-time, mostly waiting)

1
Enable the paper account
Every Interactive Brokers account comes with a free simulated twin. It gets switched on in the account settings.
2
Match the starting cash
The simulated cash is set to the same $50,000 the Rotation Plan uses. If the balances differ, the results are not comparable, and comparing is the whole point.
3
Install the bridge software
IB Gateway is a small program that runs on the computer and connects the scanner to Interactive Brokers. The paper account and the live account use different connection ports, which is literally the switch between pretend and real money.

Phase 1: Manual paper trading (can start almost immediately)

Nothing needs to be built to start collecting real validation data. The scanner already says what to do each morning.

1
Follow the morning plan by hand
The scanner runs each morning and produces its Rotation Plan. The buys and sells are placed by hand in the paper account, exactly as the plan says: buy on the planned day, hold through the ex-date, sell once the price is back to even.
2
Log five numbers per trade
Planned buy price vs. actual fill, planned sell day vs. actual sell day, the dividend received, the net result, and the scanner's predicted net. That log becomes the Results section below.

This starts producing answers in the first week, while the automation in Phase 2 is built in parallel.

Phase 2: The automation (the real Stage 2 build)

Built and tested against the paper account, piece by piece:

1
Connector
The scanner connects to Interactive Brokers, reads the account balance, and sees the current positions.
2
Order placement
The day's Rotation Plan is read automatically and the buy order is placed for the scheduled trade. It starts with simple limit orders; smarter algorithmic order types can be added later to chase better fills.
3
Exit monitor
The position is checked every day and the sell is placed once the price is back to break even. This is also where smarter exit logic can live later, such as holding a little longer when the trend is still moving up instead of selling at the first break-even moment.
4
Automatic trade log
Every fill is written down automatically: predicted vs. actual, with the gap. No memory, no spreadsheet discipline required.
5
Safety rails
The boring but critical part: what happens if an order does not fill before the ex-date cutoff (cancel, do not chase), if the data sources disagree on a price, or if the computer was off that morning.

Phase 3: Evaluate and decide (after 1 to 2 months of completed trades)

1
Compare against the prediction
The actual net per trade is held up against the predicted range (the 35% and 25% tax columns).
2
Explain every gap
Each miss gets a cause: the spread, slippage, a slow rebound, a dividend change. Costs that turn out to be real and repeatable get built into the scanner's math so the predictions stay honest.
3
The decision gate
If the actual results land inside or near the predicted range, the switch flips to real money, starting small. If they consistently miss, the flaw was found for free, and it gets fixed or rethought before any real dollars, and especially before any borrowed ones.
The one dependency: Phase 0 needs an Interactive Brokers account to exist. Everything after that can proceed without risking a cent.

🎯 What Success Looks Like

  • One to two months of trades executed exactly as the Rotation Plan scheduled them.
  • The actual net results land inside the predicted range (the 35% to 25% tax columns), or close enough that the gap is explained and priced in.
  • No surprises that the scanner could have known about: every miss gets a cause and, where possible, a fix.

Only after that does it make sense to commit real capital, and especially borrowed capital, where a flaw would cost real interest on top of real losses.

An honest caveat: a month or two of paper trading is a small sample. It cannot prove the strategy works in every market. What it can do is catch the practical flaws that backtesting is blind to, cheaply, before they cost anything.
